How to use this plan

Home kit: a sturdy table, a door, a towel, a bedsheet, a chair/couch, a stair, a backpack you fill with books or water bottles, and two filled water bottles/jugs. That's it.

Safety check: before table rows, lean on the table edge hard; it must not tip or slide. For door rows, stand on the side where the door opens away from you, so pulling pushes it into the frame. Lock it if you can.

Tracking box: log reps (or the seconds you held) on bodyweight moves. On backpack/jug moves, log the kg. Weigh the bag on a bathroom scale.

Progress without weights: add reps up to the top of the range, then make it harder: walk your feet further under the table, add books to the bag, lower slower (3 s down), or pause 2 s at the squeeze.

Order: Do the days in sequence (1→2→3→4) with a rest day whenever you need one — e.g. Mon/Tue/Thu/Fri. As a beginner, never stress about hitting all 4 perfectly; 3 solid weeks beats 4 burnt-out ones.

Sets: Tap a numbered square each time you finish a set. Weight: type what you used — it's remembered and pre-filled next time, so you can chase small jumps.

Progress: When every move's last weight feels easy for the top of the rep range, add a little (~2.5 kg lower body, ~1–2 kg upper). That's the whole game.

Rest: ~60–90s between sets. Use the timer button. Warm up 3–4 min on a bike/rower + 1 light set of the first exercise.

Rest: ~45–60s between sets. Warm up 3 min: arm circles, cat-cows, 10 bodyweight squats, 20 s marching in place.

Why these muscles?

WCS (leader): the dance is body-led — connection comes from your core and lats, not your arms. Add glutes/hamstrings for the anchor & grounded "settle," legs + ankles for balance and redirections, and postural back/rear delts for a tall frame.

Looks (fastest payoff): the V-taper — lateral delts (shoulder width), lats (the flare), upper chest, plus arms and a tight core.

Home version: lats are the hardest muscle to train without a bar, so they get two dedicated days (table rows, door rows, towel/prone pulldowns). Core work shows up every single day, and it focuses on anti-rotation and bracing (dead bug, bird dog, shoulder taps), because a leader's job is to keep the center still and let it carry the lead.

The overlap (lats, core, shoulders, glutes/legs) is why a handful of compound lifts covers both at once.
